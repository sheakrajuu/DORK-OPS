START HERE / FIELD GUIDE

A reliable scan, step by step

Read the official Nmap manual ↗

Nmap (Network Mapper) discovers hosts and examines ports and services. Treat a scan as a scoped measurement: choose only the checks you need, understand the traffic they create, then validate findings with the system owner.

01

Confirm scope

Get written approval for exact hostnames, addresses, ports, time windows, and rate limits. Resolve names and check the final target list before sending probes.

02

Discover carefully

Start with a host-discovery check when appropriate. Firewalls may block discovery probes, so “host seems down” is not proof that a system is offline.

03

Choose a narrow scan

Specify only the ports and scan method needed. Add service/version or OS detection only when authorized; these send additional probes and can be inconclusive.

04

Save and interpret

Keep a dated report in an approved location. Port states describe what Nmap observed from your scan position, not the complete security posture of a service.

How to read common port states

openAn application accepted a connection or responded to a probe; verify the service and intended exposure.

closedThe host responded, but no application was listening on that port at scan time.

filteredFiltering prevented Nmap from deciding whether the port was open or closed.

open|filteredNmap could not distinguish an open port from one whose probes were filtered, common with UDP.
